Understanding Sibling College & School Count

This question identifies other children in your household who are either currently in school and planning for college, or actively attending college now.

Examples of Included Siblings:

  • Future College Student: A younger sibling currently in 5th grade who will need college funding in the future.
  • Active College Student: An older sibling who is currently a college junior graduating next year.

Counting all siblings ensures your overall family savings capacity is fairly allocated across each child's individual timeline.

How Total Contribution Splitting Works

When set to Yes, your total monthly family savings capacity is evenly divided among all children.

Example Split Calculation:

If your total family savings capacity is $300/month and you have 2 siblings (3 children total), each child receives a baseline contribution of $100/month.

Flexibility Note:

This baseline split is just an initial default. You can fully customize, override, or boost monthly contribution rates on a month-by-month basis in Step 4 (529 Planner)!

IRS 529 Rules: Off-Campus & Living at Home Allowances

Under IRS Code Section 529(e)(3)(B), students enrolled at least half-time at an eligible educational institution (including Community Colleges) can use 529 distributions tax-free for room and board expenses even if they do not live in official campus housing.

1. Off-Campus Apartment Allowance ($8,000/yr base)

The tax-free 529 withdrawal amount for off-campus rent and food & groceries is capped at the official "Room and Board Cost of Attendance (COA)" standard set by the college's financial aid office for off-campus students.

2. Living with Parents Boarding Allowance ($4,000/yr base)

Even when living at home, the IRS permits students to withdraw tax-free 529 funds up to the institution's official COA room and board allowance for students living with parents (covering food, groceries, and housing costs). Parents can document reasonable housing/meal reimbursements provided to the student.

Qualified 529 Covered Expenses

Under IRS guidelines, tax-free distributions from a 529 plan can be used to pay for Qualified Higher Education Expenses (QHEE) at eligible post-secondary institutions:

  • Tuition & Mandatory Fees: Full cost of enrollment and required campus student fees.
  • Room & Board: Campus dorms, meal plans, or off-campus housing/food up to the college's official COA limit.
  • Books & Required Supplies: Textbooks, lab supplies, software, and tools required for coursework.
  • Computers & Peripheral Equipment: Laptops, desktop hardware, internet access, and educational software used by the student.

Non-Qualified Personal Expenses

Expenses that are NOT eligible for tax-free 529 withdrawals. Using 529 funds for these items will trigger ordinary income tax plus a 10% IRS penalty on earnings:

  • Campus Travel & Transportation: Gas, parking passes, public transit, flights home, or vehicle upkeep.
  • Personal & Lifestyle Essentials: Clothing, laundry, phone bills, streaming subscriptions, and entertainment.
  • Health & Insurance: Health insurance policies, medical bills, and OTC medicines.
  • Application & Test Fees: SAT/ACT fees, college application fees, or graduation costs.
